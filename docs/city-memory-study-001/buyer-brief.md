# Buyer brief

## The situation (working positioning hypothesis)

This specimen hypothesizes that a Montréal hospitality or workplace interior may need local character quickly, and that the alternatives may be generic skyline photography, decorative vintage imagery, or an unstructured archive dump. This is positioning copy to test with qualified stakeholders, not validated market research or a claim about buyer demand. The proposed buyer would need a point of view that feels native to the city and can survive scrutiny from a designer, operator, and rights reviewer.

## Audience and space

Primary: a boutique hotel, lobby, corridor, workplace common area, or Montréal hospitality art programme.

Secondary: an art advisor, architect, interior designer, developer, cultural institution, or archive partner assembling a place-specific installation.

This v0 study assumes one statement wall plus a sequence or satellite grouping. It is a concept study, not a final print specification.

## Desired effect

Visitors should feel that Montréal is a lived system rather than a postcard: a city of thresholds, signs, civic rooms, infrastructure, and changing edges. The sequence should reward a second look. It should feel contemporary in its editing while preserving the grain, scale, and quiet evidence of the source material.

## Working constraints

- 12 source-linked images in three to four visual families.
- Black-and-white street views balanced by aerial/oblique views.
- One statement piece approximately 36–48 inches on the long edge; corridor pieces approximately 18–24 inches, subject to production review.
- Neutral wall, controlled glazing, and captions that remain legible at walking distance.
- Archive credit beside or in the accompanying study; no unverified coordinates or historical claims in the public narrative.
- Final output must use a production-resolution source asset after rights and image-quality confirmation.

## Budget assumptions for discussion only

These are a planning hypothesis, not a quote or promise:

- City Memory Concept Study: CAD $20,000–$60,000 over 4–6 weeks, depending on
  selection depth, approved context assets, review requirements, spatial
  applications, rights complexity, and production planning.
- Installation/image package: CAD $20,000–$80,000+ before third-party print, framing, shipping, installation, insurance, taxes, and any rights/counsel costs.
- Optional provenance/interactive layer: scoped separately after the buyer chooses the level of source evidence and public annotation.

The predecessor $3,500 Diagnostic 001 is a separate reduced-scope validation
artifact, not the default price for this concept study.

## Success criteria

1. A buyer can describe the concept and where it would live without a verbal explanation.
2. An art/design reviewer can see a coherent thesis, not twelve disconnected archive images.
3. Every selected visual has a source URL, archive reference, rights status, attribution line, and explicit claim boundary.
4. A production partner can identify what still needs confirmation before fabrication.
5. A qualified stakeholder can react to a fixed next step: a paid discovery/concept study, not an open-ended platform conversation.

## Decision the specimen is designed to support

“Would this buyer fund a bounded, source-linked Montréal image programme for a real space, and what would they need changed before commissioning it?”
