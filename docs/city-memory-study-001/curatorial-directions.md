# Curatorial directions

The directions are intentionally distinct but share one thesis: the city’s memory lives in connections and thresholds, not only in landmarks. Titles are editorial labels; the archive metadata beneath each image remains clearly marked as reported, not independently corroborated.

## 1. Street as threshold

**Records:** 10, 17, 31, 45

**Rationale:** Four ground views move from commercial intersection to civic and institutional frontage. Their value is not a single “old Montréal” scene; it is the repeated act of entering, passing, turning, and looking across a street. The set gives a lobby or corridor an arrival sequence with varied verticality and facade rhythm.

**Visual relationships:** 10 and 17 are street corridors; 31 gives a broad commercial facade; 45 supplies a civic/architectural counterpoint. All four are reviewed as `ground_street` and carry full-decode review derivatives.

**Historical/source context:** The archive reports names, dates, and cote references in the ledger. Those fields are shown as archive-reported metadata only. The public copy makes no independent claim about the named buildings.

**Spatial suitability:** 31 or 45 can anchor a statement wall; 10 and 17 work as a horizontal pair or corridor sequence. Preserve the original aspect ratios and confirm full-resolution crops before printing.

**Risks:** The names and dates may invite a viewer to read more historical certainty than the current dossier supports. Keep the “archive reports” label and do not add map pins or building-history copy until independently verified.

## 2. Commerce as memory

**Records:** 58, 105

**Rationale:** Everyday commerce is treated as a visual texture: frontage, signage, vehicles, wires, and the density of a working street. This direction avoids nostalgia by focusing on the visual infrastructure of exchange.

**Visual relationships:** 58 is a tall, tightly framed storefront view; 105 opens laterally into signs, traffic, and a forecourt. Their differing orientations make a strong diptych or a vertical feature paired with a wide companion.

**Historical/source context:** The archive reports the titles, dates, and cotes. Visible signs are not promoted as independently verified brand or entity claims in this package.

**Spatial suitability:** Best as a hospitality corridor, café/bar approach, or workplace commons where guests can stand close enough to inspect detail.

**Risks:** Sign legibility can be mistaken for entity verification; copy stays at “visible signage” and “archive-reported title.” Production must confirm whether any crop or sharpening changes the source evidence.

## 3. The city from above

**Records:** 4501, 7929, 9844, 11836

**Rationale:** Aerial and oblique views shift the viewer from street participant to pattern reader. They reveal urban grain, open space, routes, and edges as visual relationships—not as a claim to exact geolocation or measurement.

**Visual relationships:** 4501 and 7929 form a high-contrast pair; 9844 supplies a more diagrammatic vertical view; 11836 provides a wider oblique movement frame. All are reviewed as aerial image modes and accepted for bounded visual use.

**Historical/source context:** Dates, when present, are reported archive metadata. The current accepted claim is the reviewed image mode and visible composition, not a location, acreage, or land-use interpretation.

**Spatial suitability:** A statement wall, stair landing, or digital layer where the viewer can move between macro pattern and street-level detail.

**Risks:** Aerial imagery naturally invites “this is here” claims. No map overlays, coordinates, measurements, or named sites should appear in buyer-facing copy until a separate source-authority pass closes them.

## 4. Water / movement

**Records:** 12115, 13272

**Rationale:** The final direction uses edge conditions and crossings as a quieter coda: movement through the city, not a monument at the end of it. It provides a visual breath after the denser street and infrastructure sequences.

**Visual relationships:** 12115 is a vertically composed oblique view; 13272 supplies a broad bridge/water composition. The pairing can close a corridor or sit opposite a lobby threshold.

**Historical/source context:** The archive reports the available filenames/titles and source URLs. The narrative uses only visible water/structure/route relationships; it does not name a location unless a future verification pass supports it.

**Spatial suitability:** Strong as a closing pair, quiet lounge grouping, or interpretive wall with a short “look again” prompt.

**Risks:** 12115’s dossier has no reported date; 13272’s date appears in the archive-reported title/date field. Keep the distinction explicit.

## Recommended v0 edit

Use **Street as threshold** as the buyer-facing lead, **Commerce as memory** as the human-scale middle, and **The city from above** as the spatial counterpoint. Keep **Water / movement** as an optional closing pair. The full 12-record set is the evidence base; a paid concept study would narrow it after buyer feedback and production-resolution review.
