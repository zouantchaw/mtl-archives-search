# Interview guide — City Memory Study 001

Use a 30–40 minute conversation. Do not pitch before the problem questions. Keep detailed notes private; the public ledger stores categorical codes and private reference tokens only.

## Opening and qualification (3 minutes)

“I’m testing whether this kind of source-linked Montreal archive study solves a current planning, cultural-programming, or space-design problem. I’m not asking you to endorse the idea, and I will record only redacted notes.”

- What is your role in selecting artwork, interpretation, archive programming, or visitor/guest experience?
- What current or upcoming space/programme would this be relevant to?
- Who owns the budget and who else must approve it?
- May I take notes? If recording is useful, may I record audio/video? Where may the private reference be stored?

Disqualify a slot if there is no active need or no decision/procurement access. Record `ineligible`, not a flattering summary.

## Problem before solution (10 minutes)

- Tell me about the last time you sourced historical/cultural visual material for this kind of space or programme.
- What was difficult, slow, risky, or disappointing?
- What do you use today instead? Who does the work, how long does it take, and what does it cost?
- What happens if the current approach stays unchanged this year?
- Which matters most: curation, visual quality, rights/attribution, historical defensibility, installation readiness, or an interactive layer? Why?
- What budget line or procurement route would normally pay for this?

Ask for an example and a recent action whenever possible. “That sounds interesting” is an opinion, not a problem signal.

## Specimen reaction (10 minutes)

Use the fixed walkthrough. Ask after each stage:

- What would you use here, if anything?
- What is unclear or untrustworthy?
- What would prevent this from being shown to your guest/client/board/archive partner?
- What would you need changed before spending time or budget?

Do not lead with the technology. If asked, explain that Provenance is the source, rights, evidence-boundary, and repeatability method behind the output.

## Offer test (5 minutes)

Read the canonical `offer_text` from [proposal.v1.json](./proposal.v1.json) exactly, including price, timing, exclusions, and payment schedule. Then ask:

- Is this the right next step for the problem you described?
- What, specifically, would stop you from approving it?
- Who would need to see a written scope?
- What is the next procurement action, and by what date?

Do not accept “I’d be interested” as a yes. Ask for a written yes, SOW review, procurement initiation, or paid deposit; log the strongest actual signal only.

## Close (2 minutes)

“What did I miss? Who else has this problem? May I send the one-page scope for your internal review?” Confirm the follow-up permission, the next step, and any request to delete notes/recording.
